# /40 sleeve wear — how Drew wants it

Every record on drewroper.com/40 gets a baked "worn sleeve": the cover as a crisp flatbed scan of a
well-played vinyl sleeve. Generated by `scripts/make-wear.py` into `assets/40/worn/`. This file is the
taste behind it, signed off on the first 21 records (Sep 23 2026). Apply it to every new record.

## Adding a record (Days 21–40)

1. Give it a `no` and `date` in `data/albums.json` (the generator only touches numbered days + the bonus).
2. `python3 scripts/make-wear.py --only <slug> --levels <picker dir>` — it gets the global defaults.
3. Show Drew low / med / high, set `wear.level` to the pick, then tune with the per-record knobs below
   from their notes. Re-run, commit, push both branches.

## Per-record knobs (`wear` in albums.json)

| key       | default | what it does |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| `level`   | `med`   | `low` / `med` / `high` — the same wear, more or less of it |
| `edge`    | 1.3     | edge wear: frost along the cut edges, wider crushed card strip and corners; above ~2 a broad, patchy rub along every edge, heaviest at the corners. On pale stock it reads as handling grime. "More edge wear" ≈ 2.5, "much more" ≈ 3.5–4 |
| `surface` | 1.0     | life on the face: near-straight scratches in every direction, broken hairlines, rub patches, a soft fold, flaked patches. "More scratching / abrasion" ≈ 2.2–2.4; the Kinks (oldest) 2.8 |
| `bends`   | 0       | creases where the sleeve got bent: a thin, broken crack line along an edge a little way in or across a corner, sometimes doubled. 2–3 is plenty |
| `fold`    | 0       | a pressed-flat dog-ear at one corner, lit like a scan: broken white crack, soft shadow, the flap a touch lighter; mirrored on the back. For busy art that swallows `bends` (Wondrous Bughouse 1.2). `fold_at`: `tl` / `tr` / `bl` / `br` (default `tr`, where the Incubus crease sits) |
| `amount`  | 1.2     | overall multiplier |
| `back`    | 1.0     | the back's share of the wear. A stark white back shows every mark: Wondrous Bughouse .5. The `fold` carries through regardless |
| `lock`    | —       | `true` = render with base settings, exactly as signed off (Incubus, Phish) |

Busy all-over art (Merriweather, Wondrous Bughouse) hides wear in the middle: push `edge` (3.5–4) instead, and if it still reads clean give it a `fold`; thin `bends` vanish in busy art.

## What reads as real (Drew's notes, in their words where possible)

- Crisp and photographic, like a scan, never blurry, muddy, cloudy or "watercolory".
- Worn ink = crisp torn flakes of paper showing through. Never translucent grey washes.
- Rings: thin, imperfect, broken, heavier where the disc sits; sometimes broader; not every sleeve has one.
- Edges: thin chipped card line (the "v4 edges"), frost along the edge, beat-up corners. Lots of notes asked
  for *more* edge wear; nobody ever asked for less.
- Folds / bends: thin broken crease lines like the Incubus top-right corner. NOT a band of flaked ink
  beside the fold (that reads as rubbed ink).
- Scratches: near-straight. Curled hairlines on black read as "dog hair".
- Cracks: the TEB right edge is the reference (Drew: "a moment of brilliance"). A hairline that starts at the cut edge, runs in across the dark field, branches once or twice, breaks into dashes where the ink held, and fades out. Thin, crisp, pale, never a band. It comes from the edge-crack pass (short hairlines in from an edge), so it's seeded per record: don't reroll a sleeve that has one.
- Light covers: paper tooth, handling grime at the edges, heavier wear than dark covers; no brown clouds.
- Older records more worn; never utterly destroyed; every sleeve different.
- Never: speckle / salt noise, grease spots, sticker residue, foxing dots, pencil-hatching striations.
- Corners are rounded at 2.8% of the width (CSS and story renders); edge wear follows that curve.
- Make subtle moves when tuning; don't swing the whole look.

## Favourites to match
Incubus *Morning View* (high, locked), Phish *A Live One* (med, locked), and Third Eye Blind (high, global defaults: Drew's favourite so far). On a dark, saturated cover like TEB's red, the frost along the edges and the ring's broken arc do the work; resist adding more.
